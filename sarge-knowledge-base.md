## Sealcoating — Basics
**Q: What is sealcoating?** A: Sealcoating is a protective coat applied over asphalt. It seals the surface against water, UV rays, gas, and oil — the stuff that breaks blacktop down. Think of it as sunscreen and a raincoat for your driveway.

**Q: Why should I sealcoat my driveway?** A: Buffalo weather is brutal on asphalt — sun, rain, and freeze-thaw cycles crack and dry it out. Sealcoating slows all of that down, keeps it flexible, and makes it look brand-new black again.

**Q: How often should I sealcoat?** A: For most residential driveways, every 2–3 years. High-traffic commercial lots sometimes go yearly. We'll tell you straight whether yours actually needs it yet — we don't upsell.

**Q: Does sealcoating fix cracks?** A: No — sealcoat is a thin protective layer, not a filler. Cracks get hot-pour rubber crack filling FIRST, then the sealcoat goes over the top. Order matters.

**Q: Is sealcoating worth it?** A: A driveway is one of the biggest surfaces on your property. Sealing it every couple years costs a fraction of resurfacing or replacing it down the road. It's cheap insurance for expensive asphalt.

**Q: What does sealcoat protect against?** A: UV/sun fade, water intrusion, freeze-thaw cracking, and gas/oil drips that eat into asphalt. It also makes the surface easier to clean and clear of snow.

**Q: Will it make my driveway look new again?** A: Yes — a fresh seal brings back that deep, even jet-black finish and sharp edges. Curb appeal jumps the day we're done.

## New Asphalt
**Q: Can I sealcoat brand-new asphalt?** A: Not right away. Fresh asphalt needs to cure and release its oils first — usually 6–12 months. Seal too early and it won't bond. We'll tell you when it's ready.

**Q: How long after new asphalt before I seal it?** A: Generally 6–12 months, depending on the season it was laid and the weather since. When in doubt, ask us to take a look.

## Process & Prep
**Q: How do you prep before sealing?** A: We clear the surface, blow off all dirt and debris, knock down any weeds in the cracks, fill the cracks with hot-pour rubber, treat oil spots so the sealer bonds, and cut clean edges. Prep is 80% of a job that lasts.

**Q: Do you fill the cracks first?** A: Always. Hot-pour rubberized crack filler goes in before any sealer. Sealing over open cracks just hides the problem — we fix it.

**Q: How many coats do you apply?** A: Typically two coats for full coverage and durability. Heavily worn surfaces may need extra attention in spots.

**Q: How do you apply the sealer?** A: Spray and/or squeegee depending on the surface, with hand-cut edges around garages, lawns, and borders for a crisp finish.

**Q: Do I need to do anything before you arrive?** A: Just move vehicles, bikes, and anything else off the driveway, and keep pets and kids clear on the day. We handle the rest.

## Timing & Weather
**Q: What's the best time of year to sealcoat in Buffalo?** A: Late spring through early fall, when temps are reliably warm and dry. That's prime sealing season in WNY.

**Q: What temperature do you need to sealcoat?** A: Generally 50°F and rising, dry, with no rain in the forecast for about 24 hours. Sealer needs warmth and sun to cure right.

**Q: Can you seal if it's going to rain?** A: No — we need a dry window of roughly 24 hours so the sealer can cure. Rain on fresh sealer ruins it, so we watch the forecast and schedule around it.

**Q: How late in the year can you sealcoat?** A: Through the fall, as long as days stay warm enough. Once it turns cold, sealing season is over and we switch to snow and salt.

**Q: Can you sealcoat in winter?** A: No — it's too cold to cure. Winter is our plowing, salting, and walkway season. Get on the list early in spring instead.

## Curing & Aftercare
**Q: How long before I can walk on it?** A: Usually a few hours, but give it until it's fully dry to be safe — we'll tell you on the day based on weather.

**Q: How long before I can drive or park on it?** A: Plan on staying off it for 24–48 hours. Cooler or humid days take longer. We'll give you the exact green light.

**Q: Will the sealer track into my garage or onto shoes?** A: Once it's cured, no. Stay off it during the cure window and you're good.

**Q: How do I take care of it after?** A: Keep it clean, clean up gas/oil drips quickly, and don't turn your tires while parked for the first few days. That's about it.

**Q: Can it rain after you seal?** A: It needs to cure first (about 24 hours). After that, rain is no problem — that's the whole point of sealing.

## Crack Filling
**Q: What is crack filling?** A: We rout/clean the cracks and fill them with hot-pour rubberized sealant that flexes with temperature changes. It stops water from getting in and freezing.

**Q: Why is crack filling important?** A: Water in a crack freezes, expands, and tears your asphalt apart over a Buffalo winter. Filling cracks early is the cheapest way to add years to your pavement.

**Q: Can you just fill cracks without sealcoating?** A: Yes — we can do crack filling on its own if that's all you need.
